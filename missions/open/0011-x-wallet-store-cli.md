## Summary

Turns `WalletStore` from a stub into something the CLI can actually provision against. Two deliverables:

1. **A provisioning path.** Five new `octo identity` subcommands — `register`, `select`, `list`, and the two halves of the rotation lifecycle, `rotate complete` and `rotate abort` — plus unlock threading on the existing `whoami`, `show`, `rotate`, and `revoke`. Without `register` the store is always empty, so `octo whoami` keeps exiting 2 and nothing an operator can see changes. `rotate` alone does not complete a rotation, and `begin_rotation` on its own is the defect this RFC found: an operator who starts a rotation and cannot finish or abandon it is worse off than one who never started, because the identity sits in `Rotating` with a grace window running and no path out of it.
2. **The migration.** 13 real `WalletStore::open()` call sites across five CLI modules, and separately **26** sites that reach for the identity key, currently consume the stub's unconditional `NotActive`. Each must be classified as _metadata_ (no unlock) or _signing_ (unlock required) and migrated accordingly, and the deprecated sentinel must then be deleted.

   The two populations are **not** the same set, and the split between them is not the obvious one. The 13 are the sites that open the store. The 26 key-reaching sites divide again:

   | Route                                                                             | Sites | Deprecation warnings                                             |
   | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
   | Direct `octo_wallet::active_identity(&store)`                                     | 15    | 15                                                               |
   | Indirect, via `common::resolve_active_identity_key()`                             | 2     | 0 — the helper is defined once and calls the sentinel internally |
   | Indirect, via `common::resolve_active_did()` (a thin projection over the above)   | 5     | 0 — same funnel; the warning fires once inside the deeper helper |
   | Indirect, via `governance::resolve_active_did()` (local mirror of the agent pair) | 1     | 0 — the local helper opens the store itself                      |
   | Indirect, via `vault::active_owner_did()` (local helper)                          | 3     | 0 — the local helper opens the store itself                      |

   Per module, the direct 15 are identity 6, capability 4, governance 3, agent 1, vault 1. The 11 indirect sites are 7 in `agent.rs`, 1 in `governance.rs`, and 3 in `vault.rs`.

   The funnel is the part that matters. `common::resolve_active_identity_key()` opens the store and then calls the sentinel, so its two direct CLI handler callers reach the key without naming it, and `common::resolve_active_did()` is a thin projection over that helper, so its five CLI handler callers also reach the key without ever calling `octo_wallet::active_identity` themselves. The local mirrors — `governance::resolve_active_did()` (a clone that does not depend on the agent module's private `mod common`) and `vault::active_owner_did()` (a local helper that opens the store independently) — funnel another four: `governance.rs:358` reaches the key through the governance local, and `vault.rs:1064`, `vault.rs:1125`, and `vault.rs:1315` reach it through the vault local. The deprecation warning fires once inside each helper's body — which means the sentinel's build-time signal reaches 15 of 26 sites, and a sweep that trusts the warning count as the migration count will leave eleven sites unmigrated while every warning is accounted for. AC-1 and `tv_x_c_11` are specified against the 26 precisely because the 15 is the number the compiler reports and the 26 is the number that has to be migrated. One of the 15 sits inside a `#[cfg(test)]` block and is carried in both counts for the same reason it is carried in the work list: it compiles under `cargo test` and it warns, so leaving it behind breaks the test build.

The classification sweep is the first acceptance criterion and it is not a formality. The split is what keeps this tractable: metadata readers need no passphrase, so most sites should not gain one. Guessing the distribution wrong in either direction is expensive — over-classifying puts a prompt on read-only commands, and under-classifying leaves a signing path reading metadata and failing at the point of use.

**The passphrase column is derived from the receiver, not from the need, and the rule is stated
here because the two are not the same axis.** A method on `WalletStore` takes no passphrase. A
method on `UnlockedWallet` requires one, because `UnlockedWallet`'s fields are private and the
specified API offers no constructor that takes an already-decrypted key — the only way to hold
one is to have called `unlock`. This matters most for `complete_rotation` and `abort_rotation`,
which is where the two axes visibly part company. `abort_rotation` seals nothing, appends no
successor record, and takes no passphrase of its own, so a reader applying this mission's own
§Summary principle — metadata readers need no passphrase, and over-classifying puts a prompt on
a command that does not need one — would classify both as prompt-free and be wrong. They prompt
because they are reached through the handle, not because they read key material. An implementer
adding a method to either receiver should classify from the receiver, and can check the
classification by asking whether the method could be called without an `UnlockedWallet` in hand
— if it could, it does not prompt.

**This table is the single source of truth for exit codes, and it lives in RFC-0011-x §CLI dispatch.** It is restated here for the reader who opens only this file, and a divergence between the two is itself a defect. An earlier revision of this table carried `0, 2, 64` for `register`, `0, 4, 64` for `select`, and `0, 2, 4, 92, 64` for `revoke` — three rows each missing codes the RFC assigned — and the failure mode is specific: **a mission that is silent about a code is read as a mission that says the code cannot occur.** An implementer working from this file alone would have shipped three handlers with no `NoOctoHome` arm, no `AlreadyRevoked` arm, and no `InvalidStateTransition` arm on a path that can reach all three.

The 6 on `select` is the one cell in this table that its own surrounding prose contradicted, and it is recorded here so the next reader does not remove it again. `select` on a revoked record returns `NotActive { current_state: Revoked }`, which the translation table reuses as `OctoCliError::AlreadyRevoked` at exit 6, the same exit `register` reaches through a different substrate variant. The row previously read `0, 2, 4, 64`, omitting the 6, and the prose was split rather than uniformly wrong: one site said 6 occurs on `select` and four said 2, because the arms list and the not-interchangeable paragraph both taught that the whole `NotActive` family is exit 2, which is true of the bare arm and false of the revoked one. The substrate mission's AC-26 asserted the `NotActive` return and its vector asserted the `select` reachability, so the path was specified and the table was the only artifact that did not carry it. An implementer building from the table would have shipped `select` with no `AlreadyRevoked` arm, and the missing arm is on the guard that keeps a terminal record unselectable — the A17 adversary this amendment exists to answer. The 6 was added to `select` in both copies of this table, the four prose sites were corrected to discriminate, and the two copies of the table remain byte-identical.

Two more cells in this table were checked against the rules stated below it rather than against each other, and each was wrong in the direction its own rule forbids. `select` omitted **27** although 27 is universal for every row, because every `octo` command resolves home before it opens anything and `select` opens the store. `whoami` carried **43** although 43 belongs to every row that touches a lifecycle transition, and `active_identity` reads a key rather than moving one: the five refusals behind 43 are returned by `register` and the three rotation methods, none of which is on the whoami path. Both cells are corrected here and in the companion mission, and the exit-code columns remain byte-identical across the two copies.

The four universal codes, and why every row carries them: **27** (`NoOctoHome`) because every `octo` command routes through `octo-cli/src/home.rs::resolve` before it opens anything, and that resolver fails closed on an empty `OCTO_HOME` and on a missing `HOME` — the 27 is produced **upstream of the store**, not by it. **64** because every row has a generic arm. **43** on every row that touches a lifecycle transition. **92** on every row that needs the identity key, plus `register` because a seal can fail on an unwritable or corrupt vault, which is a locked store from the operator's side.

Six rows carry substrate-faithfulness corrections that an earlier draft got wrong, and each was checked against `IdentityAction` in `crates/octo-cli/src/commands/identity.rs` rather than inferred:

- `register` routes to **`WalletStore::register`**, not `UnlockedWallet::register`. This is the correction that matters, because the earlier routing was not merely a naming slip — it was unsatisfiable. `register` was placed on `UnlockedWallet`, the only way to obtain one is `unlock`, and the only way to `unlock` is to already have a sealed slot, so on an empty store the bootstrap command could not run at all. The `Passphrase` column is still a passphrase and the reason is now stated in the cell: `register` needs one to **seal** the new slot. Sealing and unlocking are different operations that happen to share a parameter, and conflating them is what produced the deadlock. It also takes a **seed file** rather than generating in-process, because the operator guide's onboarding step already writes a 0600 seed file via `octo-wallet init --seed-out` — composing with that step costs one added command in the guide rather than a rewritten section, and passing a freshly generated seed through the same path covers in-process generation.
- `select` routes to `WalletStore::select`, **not** `UnlockedWallet::select`. Moving the active pointer is a pure index write that touches no key material, so a passphrase there would be exactly the over-classification this mission's own Summary warns against — a prompt on a command that reads and writes one field. `register` and `select` are the two store methods that take `&mut self`, read no active key, and need no unlock; they sit together for that reason.
- `show`'s `<did>` is **optional** in the substrate (`Show { did: Option<String> }`, defaulting to the active identity), so the dispatch table must show it in brackets.
- `revoke` takes a **required `--reason` flag**, not a positional. An earlier draft of this table listed it with no argument at all, which would have shipped a subcommand invocation that cannot parse.
- `rotate` carries **3** (`AlreadyRotating`), **5** (`HsmUnavailable`), and **11** (`SigningFailed`) — the three the successor-proof path can produce, and an arm list without them routes every one of them to exit 64 with a "report the diagnostic" message an operator can do nothing with. It does **not** carry 6, and neither does `revoke`; the reason is measured and is in §New error variants.
- `rotate complete` and `rotate abort` are new because the substrate's second half of the lifecycle is. `begin_rotation` writes `rotation_started_at_unix_secs` and moves the key to `Rotating`; without a way to finish or abandon that, the only exit from `Rotating` is `revoke`, which is a different operation with a different consequence. Both unlock, and neither seals — `begin_rotation` already sealed the successor slot.

**`complete_rotation` takes a clock argument, and the grace period is 24 hours.** The substrate signature is `complete_rotation(&mut self, now_unix_secs: u64)`, and it refuses with `GracePeriodNotElapsed` until `ROTATION_GRACE_PERIOD_SECS` — 24 hours — have elapsed since the recorded start. The CLI therefore supplies the wall clock, and **the overwhelmingly common outcome of `octo identity rotate complete` is a refusal**, not a success. That is the correct behaviour and the command must exist anyway, but it has an operational consequence this mission owns: the refusal has to reach the operator as exit 43 with the remaining hours in the message, because an operator who runs `rotate complete` an hour after `rotate` and sees exit 64 with "report the diagnostic" will report a bug rather than wait. `abort_rotation` takes no clock and is unconditional within `Rotating`.

### New error variants (Layer C)

**Three** new `OctoCliError` variants, at **slots 92, 93, and 94** — the first three of the band RFC-0011-h G3 reserved above the 91 high-water mark. An earlier revision of this section said `ONE`, and the count was wrong because the same revision that found the first unmapped error failed to keep looking: the six `WalletError` outcomes with no CLI arm are not one, and the finding that produced slot 92 was itself the reason slot 93 became visible. The second revision reached `TWO` and then hit the same message-versus-code split a second time. The rule that follows from both misses is the one this section now enforces, and it is worth stating first because it is what the third slot is for: **a shared exit code is free, a shared message is not.** A named arm is required wherever the rendered text would otherwise be wrong, and only the exit code is shareable.

Slot 92, `WalletLocked` — the first slot above the 91 high-water mark:

```rust
/// The wallet store is locked and the operation needs the identity key.
#[error("wallet store is locked: unlock with a passphrase to continue")]
WalletLocked,
```

Slot 93, `IdentityTransitionRefused` — for the five lifecycle refusals:

```rust
/// The identity lifecycle refused the requested transition. Carries the
/// substrate's own reason, sanitized, and no key material.
#[error("identity lifecycle refused this transition: {reason}")]
IdentityTransitionRefused { reason: String },
```

Slot 94, `WeakPassphrase` — exit 2, and a message of its own:

```rust
/// A supplied passphrase is below the enforced floor. The reason is a fixed
/// string naming the floor, never a fragment of the supplied passphrase.
#[error("passphrase rejected: {reason}")]
WeakPassphrase { reason: String },
```

| `VaultSlotNotFound`                          | `WalletLocked` (slot 92)                       | 92   |
| `VaultDecryptionFailed` — a wrong passphrase | `WalletLocked` (slot 92)                       | 92   |

Four of those rows are corrections, and each was checked against the exit map in `crates/octo-cli/src/error.rs` rather than reasoned about:

- **`Config` deliberately has no arm.** RFC-0011-x §Home resolution step 4 carried the measurement: `Config` is a stringly-typed catch-all with **sixteen** construction sites in `crates/octo-wallet/src/vault.rs`, of which one is a directory error and **fifteen** are crypto or serialisation failures, plus four more in `agent.rs` for a poisoned mutex. An earlier revision of this mission mapped it to exit 27 — `NoOctoHome`, whose message is _"set $OCTO_HOME or $HOME before running this command"_ — which would have told an operator with a full disk to change an environment variable that is already correct. It is also an arm with no reachable case: `octo-cli/src/home.rs::resolve` rejects an empty `OCTO_HOME` and a missing `HOME` **before** any command opens the store, so exit 27 is already produced upstream with an accurate message. The obligation this mission now carries on `Config` is to write **no arm**, and `tv_x_c_14` asserts the absence by rendering a crypto-failure `Config` and checking the operator is not told to set a variable.
- **`WeakPassphrase` shares exit 2 and not a sentence.** An earlier revision proposed reusing an existing exit-2 variant and spending nothing. All **seven** variants already at exit 2 render a sentence about something else: `NoActiveIdentity` says there is no active identity when the identity is selected and present, `ConfirmationRequired` asks for a flag, `AuditorDenied` names an auditor, `ClapParse` names a syntax error, `InvalidRoleSlug` names a role, and `NoAnchorVerifyInMode` and `InvalidProposalState` name states unrelated to a passphrase. Reusing any of them would print a false message on **every** occurrence — the same laundering as the `Config` row above, at a second site. So slot 94 buys the message and the exit stays 2, because the codes are a surface for scripts and a script that branches on 2 branches on "refused", not on "which refusal". What it must not do is exit 92: 92 says the store is locked and to try again, and an operator who retries a twelve-character passphrase forever will never succeed.
- **`InvalidStateTransition` was considered for the five refusals and rejected on measurement, not taste.** It already exits 43, so the slot argument is neutral — but its `Display` is hard-wired to agents: it names `AgentState`, cites RFC-0015-a, and says the valid edges are `registered -> running` and `running -> terminated`. Reusing it for a wallet rotation would print an agent state machine at an operator who is trying to rotate a key. This is the rule stated at the top of the section, arrived at a second time, which is the reason it is stated there.
- **`WalletError::IdentityNotFound` maps to exit 4** via the **existing** `OctoCliError::IdentityNotFound(String)`. No new slot is spent, because the parent RFC's `octo identity show` exit table already reserves exit 4 for that case, and the existing variant already renders the DID. The two variants are unrelated types that happen to share a name; this row is the mapping between them, and no artifact may refer to a "no-such-identity" variant — no such variant exists.

`revoke` does **not** carry 6 in the dispatch table, and an earlier revision of that table said it did. The claim was that `WalletError::AlreadyRevoked` is what the state machine returns on a second revoke; it is not. `IdentityKey::revoke` is idempotent — it returns `Ok(())` immediately when `lifecycle == Revoked` — so a second revoke exits 0. The mapping row above still exists because `register` and `select` reach the same refusal through a different path, and those two carry 6.

Passphrase acquisition follows the existing `octo-wallet` binary pattern: `rpassword` for the prompt, `--passphrase-stdin` for non-interactive contexts. **Never a command-line flag** — a passphrase in `argv` is readable by every local user through the process table. When neither a prompt nor the flag is available, the unlock fails with exit 92 rather than blocking on a hidden prompt forever in a cron job.

### Call-site migration

13 real call sites. `WalletStore::open` appears 16 times across `octo-cli`; the other 3 are doc comments, not calls — two in `commands/identity.rs` and one in `commands/agent.rs`.

| Module                   | Sites | Notes                                                  |
| ------------------------ | ----- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| `commands/identity.rs`   | 4     | `whoami`, `show`, `rotate`, `revoke`                   |
| `commands/capability.rs` | 4     | capability mint and attenuate sign with the holder key |
| `commands/governance.rs` | 3     | attest and vote sign                                   |
| `commands/vault.rs`      | 1     | classification to be established by AC-1               |
| `commands/agent.rs`      | 1     | classification to be established by AC-1               |

The `vault.rs` and `agent.rs` sites are the ones most likely to be metadata-only — vault transfer authoring and agent attach may only need the DID. AC-1 settles it against the code rather than by assumption.

Two corrections worth recording, because the earlier forms of this note were wrong in ways that would have misled the sweep.

**The doc comments.** The three doc comments were described as "two in `identity.rs` describing the `map_wallet_open_error` helper". The 3-comment count is right; the parenthetical explaining what they describe is not, and a reviewer following it would have gone looking for a helper that is not where the note said it was.

**The call-site count.** `map_wallet_open_error` is defined once and called at **4** production sites, all in `commands/identity.rs`. An earlier revision of this note claimed "7 times in `commands/identity.rs` and once in `commands/capability.rs`"; both numbers conflate textual occurrences with call sites. The 7 is the count of every mention in that file — one definition, four calls, and two references inside the sanitizer's own test — and the `capability.rs` mention is a doc comment that describes the helper without calling it. A migration that sized its work at 8 sites would have found 4.

The correct shape across the 13 `WalletStore::open()` sites: 4 route through the shared helper, 4 route through `map_capability_internal` in `commands/capability.rs`, and 5 inline the sanitization directly. All 13 sanitize, so there is no path-leak gap to close here — but that is three different spellings of the same mapping, and `unlock` adds a second, larger error surface. AC-1 should record which spelling each site uses, because thirteen hand-maintained error arms is where a `WalletError` variant added for the unlock path would be handled at some sites and dropped at others.

`WalletStore::lookup_identity_record` is a third method on the store that the parent RFC does not mention. Any call site reaching for it must be included in the sweep.

Three of these could not fail as previously written, and each named the wrong implementation that would pass:

- `tv_x_c_11` read "every one of the 18 sites is exercised by at least one vector". That is a count of the mission's own prose, so a mission that listed 18 sites and exercised 3 would satisfy it. The falsifiable form is a **source-level inventory**: 26 named call sites, and the assertion fails if any real site is absent from the list. The 26 comes from grepping, not from counting this document — the 15 the compiler reports and the 26 that has to be migrated differ by exactly the eleven that funnel through helper functions: 7 in `agent.rs` (2 via `common::resolve_active_identity_key()` and 5 via `common::resolve_active_did()`, the latter being a thin projection over the former), 1 in `governance.rs` via the local `resolve_active_did()` mirror at `governance.rs:96`, and 3 in `vault.rs` via the local `active_owner_did()` at `vault.rs:871`. The second clause — "each mapped to the vector that exercises it" — was a **self-referential** claim in a subtler form: whether a mapping is honest is a matter for a reader, not a checker, so a vector asserting it asserted the mission's own prose a second time. It is now narrowed to the part a checker can decide: every mapping target must be a vector id that **exists in this mission's set**, so a site pointing at a vector nobody wrote fails. What a mapping means is still the reviewer's call, and saying so is what stops the criterion from pretending otherwise.
- `tv_x_c_13` read "exits 92 rather than blocking". A blocking test either hangs the suite or is wrapped in a timeout that reports success on timeout, which is the opposite of what it claims. The bound is now the assertion: the run must complete **and** exit 92 within it. A hang is a failure, not a pass, and removing the no-TTY pre-flight must produce that failure rather than a slow green.
- `tv_x_c_2` covered `list` and `show` but not `select`. `select` is the one store-level command that **writes**, and it is the one a reviewer is most likely to over-classify into a prompt. Running all three with stdin closed is what makes the vector test the classification rather than two of the three read-only cases.

`tv_x_c_6` and `tv_x_c_11` are still the two that matter most. A wrong passphrase exiting 2 would be a lie — the identity exists, the operator just failed to unlock — and the guide's troubleshooting entry for exit 2 sends the operator to re-provision, which would not help.

`tv_x_c_13` covers the case that is easy to leave uncovered and expensive in production. `tv_x_c_7` tests the flag when it is present and `tv_x_c_6` tests a wrong passphrase, so between them the non-interactive-with-no-flag path — an unattended `cron` job, a CI step — has no other vector. That path either blocks forever on an invisible prompt or hangs waiting for stdin, and it is the exact case RFC-0011-x §Adversary Analysis A6 describes.

### Guide update

- [ ] **AC-25:** `--seed-file` handling is specified rather than implied, because `register` is the only command in this mission that reads a **plaintext identity seed** off the operator's disk, and nothing in the substrate decides its fate. Three obligations, in order:

  1. The file is exactly **32 bytes**. `octo-wallet init` enforces that itself and `octo identity register` must not be laxer than the command that wrote the file; a 31-byte seed is a truncated identity, not a weak one, and the error must say so rather than naming a length rule the operator cannot act on.
  2. A file whose mode is **group- or world-readable is refused**, with the mode printed. The file is written 0600 by `octo-wallet init` and the refusal costs an operator who has not touched it nothing; the alternative — reading a secret out of a file anyone on the host can read — is the same posture §Adversary Analysis A9 takes for the store root, applied to the one file that holds the identity itself. The judgement being made explicit is that a permissive mode is treated as a compromise already, not as a warning to be printed after the fact. An earlier draft read the file unconditionally and said nothing, which left the check as an implementer's private judgement rather than the mission's.
  3. The file is **not deleted**, and the success output says so. Deleting an operator's file unasked is a worse failure than leaving it, and the seed is the only recovery path for a store that is lost or rebuilt — but leaving it silently is how a plaintext identity seed ends up in a backup six months later. So the file survives and the operator is told, in the command's own output, that it survives and why.

## Notes

Filed 2026-09-30 per RFC-0011-x §Companion mission YAML pairing.

The `octo` binary is a one-shot dispatcher with no daemon and no listening socket, so a passphrase prompt per command is the whole of the operator experience. That is a real cost, and it is the reason metadata readers must stay prompt-free: an operator listing identities should not have to unlock a key they are not using.

The guide currently documents the wall in **eleven claims across seven locations** (enumerated by anchor sentence in AC-26). Six of the eleven state it outright; the other five describe it from the symptom — `init` registers nothing, `init` does not register an identity with the CLI, `role select` has no identity to select, `whoami` exits 2 with nothing to substitute — which is the same obstruction stated in different words. All eleven become actively misleading after this change: a reader who follows the exit-2 troubleshooting entry will re-provision an identity that is already registered, and a reader who follows the §4 caveat will treat a working login as a seed-generation step.

**The count was wrong six times before it was measured**, and the sequence is worth recording because each wrong number is a different mistake rather than a restatement of the one before it. Two early drafts said three. One said five. An external review of the artifacts said six. Then two reviewers of this same revision reported different numbers — one said seven and called the table a partial enumeration, the other said the count of seven verified. Every one of those was a number reached by reasoning about what ought to count rather than by enumerating what does, and the disagreement between the two reviewers is the sharpest evidence available: they were counting different units and neither could tell from the prose which unit the artifacts meant.

The resolution is that the artifacts never said which unit they were counting, and **no single number can express the answer**, because the three defensible units give three different results: comment blocks give 6, locations give 7, claims give 11. The block count is six and not seven because one of the seven locations is guide prose rather than a shell comment — the §17 exit-2 troubleshooting entry, whose two claims are the only two of the eleven that no comment run encloses — and it is six and not five because §22 step 3 and §22 step 14 are two comment runs rather than one, in the same section and a hundred and forty lines apart. Both figures were five in earlier revisions of this sentence and of the RFC's §Motivation item beside it, and both were reached the same way: by counting the blocks that carry two claims and stopping there. This mission is the one that owns the census, so it is also the one that has to be right about which unit AC-26's eleven rows collapse into, and AC-26 itself is unchanged — it is keyed on anchor sentences and never stated a block count. The claims cell read **9** while this mission's own headline, the RFC's, and the census in AC-26 all said eleven — the correction that produced eleven was applied to the headline and to the table and not to this sentence, which is the third copy of a number of which the other two were fixed together. It is recorded rather than silently overwritten because the number is load-bearing in the argument: if the claims cell were nine, the headline would be wrong by two, and if the headline is right, this cell was wrong. Neither reading survives the census, which has eleven rows. A fourth unit, lines containing the phrase `wallet store` or `WalletStore`, gives 7, and it was read as the location count, which is the coincidence that made "seven" survive five revisions looking correct. The coincidence was real and the reading was not: the census's seven locations and the sweep's seven lines are two different sevens. The sweep's seven lines fall in **5** locations, because two of them are the two claims of one §4 step 2a block, and two of the census's seven locations, the §18 step 5 pair and the §31 step 1 claim, produce no sweep line at all. What the sweep's seven lines do match is the number of claims they touch, also seven, and that is a third seven rather than a confirmation of the first. The part of this that was wrong was the explanation, not the number: a figure can coincide with another figure while measuring a different set, which is what happened here, and which is why a number that agreed with a true count for five revisions was still not that count. The two figures a reader is meant to take away are the six comment blocks and the seven locations, and the sweep is the third unit, measured against both. Two guide locations are near-misses that a broader definition sweeps in — the §0 `if ! octo whoami` guard and the §18 step 4 expectation — and a narrower one drops the three symptom-phrased claims entirely.

Two further errors repeated while writing the table itself. The first attempt made **line numbers** primary, and four of the seven had already drifted by the time it was re-measured; the guide grows, and a line anchor silently becomes a wrong criterion. The second attempt used a keyword sweep to re-derive the list and found **five**, because rows 5 and 9 state the wall in terms of `role select` having no identity and never mention the store — and the third attempt found a clean-looking **seven** that was really a line count. All three are the same error: a filter narrower than the category being counted, or a count of the wrong unit. The table survives only because it was built by reading the guide, and it stays correct only if a future sweep reads the guide rather than searching it.

The rule this mission now carries, which applies to every number in these four artifacts: **a count in prose is a claim, not a fact. Either enumerate it or key the criterion on something stable.**

**A second rule, learned the same way, about exit codes rather than counts: a shared exit code is free, a shared message is not.** It was reached twice in one sweep, independently, and the two instances have the same shape. `Config` is a catch-all whose **sixteen** construction sites in `vault.rs` are **fifteen** crypto or serialisation failures and one directory error, and mapping it to exit 27 would have printed "set `$OCTO_HOME` or `$HOME`" for a full disk. `WeakPassphrase` shares exit 2 with **seven** existing variants, and every one of them renders a sentence about roles, agents, anchors, proposals, confirmation, or a parse error. In both cases the tempting move — reuse the code, let the message be approximate — produces a _false instruction_, which is worse than a vague one: a vague message sends an operator to gather information, and a false one sends them to change something that was already correct.

The first pass spent one slot per distinct failure class and reached two. Both defects were classes that shared a code with something else. The rule to run **before** declaring a count, rather than after, is therefore: for every outcome sharing an exit code with a different outcome, ask what its `Display` would say, and if the answer is "something about the wrong thing", the code is shareable and the variant is not. Applying it moved the count from two to three, and it is stated in the RFC, in the substrate mission, and here rather than in one of them — which is the only reason the third move was caught in the same sweep instead of the next one.

**A third rule, about the correction rather than the count: a correction that replaces one enumerated number with another enumerated number has not been checked, it has only been moved.** Two instances in this mission's history, both in the same direction, and both a reviewer's claim about a number rather than a substrate's behaviour. The first was a `.rs` figure in `0102-a-wallet-foundation`, where 36 was "the recursive count" when 36 is the `src/` tree minus `lib.rs` and the recursive count is 52. The second was this round's `WalletStore::open` breakdown, where a correct per-file decomposition was "corrected" from 1 in `agent` to 2 by a count that had included the doc comment at that site's own opening — and the two totals, 13 and 14, disagreed in the direction that should have stopped the edit rather than licensed it. The working rule that falls out: **when a breakdown's parts do not sum to its stated total, the breakdown is the thing that is wrong, not the total**, because the total was verified against a different and simpler measurement. A count is not corrected because a second number appeared; it is corrected when the measurement that produced it was wrong, and re-measuring is the only thing that establishes that.

This is why the figures elsewhere in this mission are stated as unions with their parts named — 13 as 3 + 1 + 1 + 4 + 4, 26 as 15 + 2 + 5 + 1 + 3, 16 as 1 directory and 15 crypto, 7 exit-2 variants rather than 6. A union can be checked by adding it up, and a number that cannot be added up cannot be checked by anyone, including the reviewer who is about to be wrong about it again. One union carries a qualification the others do not: of the 15 direct key-reaching sites, 14 are production and one is inside a `#[cfg(test)]` block, so the production-only total is 25 and the 26 is the migration work list. The test site still compiles under `cargo test` and still emits the deprecation warning, so it is migrated with the rest.
